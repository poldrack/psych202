## Overview

Cognitive neuroscience is a very broad field. Rather than surveying all
possible findings, we will focus on exemplary research from each domain,
with the goal of understanding the kinds of questions we can and cannot
successfully address using cognitive neuroscience methods. In addition
to discussing the main content areas, we will address cross-cutting
issues related to reproducibility, ethics, and the use of AI in
neuroscience research.

## Course Structure

This course combines three interwoven elements: collaborative engagement
with primary literature, structured use and critique of AI tools, and
team-based synthesis projects that develop throughout the quarter.

### Team Project Threads

You will work in teams of 3-4 students on a quarter-long \"project
thread\" - a running synthesis document that tracks how each week\'s
content relates to a central question your team has chosen. Rather than being a 
traditional research proposal, this will be a living collaborative literature
review with critical commentary that grows as you encounter new
material.

**Week 1:** Submit your top 3 questions of interest and your
methodological background/skills.

**Week 2:** Teams announced. Meet to draft a team charter and identify 2-3 possible thread directions.

**Week 3:** Submit 1-paragraph problem statement and 5 foundational
papers.

**Weeks 4--9:** Weekly individual thread updates each Sunday, reviewing
the previous week. Each update (200-400 words) connects that week\'s
readings to your thread question - what\'s relevant, what challenges
your assumptions, what methods might apply.

**Week 10:** Polished group-authored thread document (4,000--6,000
words). You may use AI tools to generate the outline, but the text must
be written by group members; no copy/pasting of text from AI tools is allowed.

**Finals week:** Final presentation (15 min) during the final exam
period.

### Pre-Class Collaborative Annotation

All readings will be hosted on Hypothesis via Canvas. You are expected
to make substantive annotations on each reading---asking questions,
responding to peers, highlighting key claims, or noting connections to
other work. Annotations are due by 11:59 pm the night before class.
Quality matters more than quantity; to receive credit you must make at
least two thoughtful annotations per reading. *You should not use AI tools to generate your comments.* I will review annotations
before class to identify points of confusion or debate to address during the discussion.

### AI Engagement and Critique

Rather than prohibiting AI tools in general, this course treats critical AI
engagement as a skill to develop.

This will include:

- **AI as Foil:** For selected readings, generate an AI
  summary/explanation, then identify what it gets wrong, oversimplifies,
  or misses. Document \"hallucinations\" where AI confidently states
  things that aren\'t in the paper or are neuroscientifically incorrect.

- **AI-Assisted Literature Search:** For your thread project, you may
  use AI to identify relevant papers or generate initial outlines---but
  you must document your AI interactions and reflect on what was useful
  versus misleading.

- **AI Limitations Log:** Throughout the quarter, maintain a brief log
  of cases where AI tools failed or succeeded in helping you understand
  the material. At the end of the quarter you will submit a reflection
  on what you learned through this log.

## Course Policies

### Team Expectations

Each team will complete a brief charter in Week 2 outlining how you will
communicate, divide work, and handle disagreements. In Week 6, I will
ask each student individually (via a brief survey) how the team is
functioning. In extreme cases, a student may petition to complete an
individual project, but this must be requested by Week 6.

### AI Tool Policy

You may use AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) to assist with literature
search, brainstorming, and drafting---but you must (1) document all AI
use in your submissions by including an "AI Acknowledgment" section, (2)
critically evaluate AI outputs, and (3) take full responsibility for the
accuracy and originality of your work. AI-generated text submitted
without attribution or critical engagement will be treated as a
violation of academic integrity.

You may use whichever AI tools you have access to.  All students can request standard-tier accounts for both [Claude](https://uit.stanford.edu/service/claude) and [ChatGPT](https://uit.stanford.edu/service/openai-chatgpt-edu), which are free to students.

### Students with Documented Disabilities

Students who may need an academic accommodation based on the impact of a
disability must initiate the request with the Office of Accessible
Education (OAE). The OAE is located at 563 Salvatierra Walk (phone:
723-1066).

### Honor Code

You are expected to uphold the Stanford Honor Code. Scholastic
dishonesty, including plagiarism and misrepresentation of AI-generated
content as your own, will not be tolerated. See the AI Tool Policy above
for specific expectations.
